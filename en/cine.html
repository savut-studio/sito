cine

A media player for live events.

Line up video, images and audio, press GO and the next one plays. On the second screen, the one facing the audience, only what you decide comes out.

[ early beta ]

01the basic idea

Simple, yet professional: made for people who have never used show-control software, with the complexity hidden until you go looking for it.

the conference

An opening sting, the guests' videos, the speakers' slides as images. Everything is a cue in the list: press GO when it's time and it goes on air.

Behind it you can keep a looping background: when a cue ends that stays, never black or the desktop.

the event

Music, films, graphics. You stay at your screen and see what's on air, what's next, and what happens at the end of each cue.

You can change the list even after the show has started: the cue on air is never touched.

One cue on air at a time: cine is a player, not VJ software. No stacked layers, no overlapping audio.

02how to read it

Left to right: Preview → GO → On air. One colour per state, throughout the app.

●

red: on air

What the audience is watching right now. Below the monitor, big, the time remaining (red in the last 10 s).

●

green: starts with GO

The next one, already open and ready. The GO button always tells you what it will start.

●

blue: selected

You've clicked it: you see it in Preview and hear it, without it going on air. Then you decide: "Make next" or "Go now".

03two ways to use it

Same cues, same engine, same file: only the view changes. You can switch even with the show running (⌘1, ⌘2).

  • List: a list in order. GO plays the next one, which advances by itself. Ideal for anything that follows a script.
  • Tiles: a grid of tiles with thumbnails. One click selects, Return or GO plays, keys 1–9 play straight away. Ideal for things you pull out when needed.

04tell me more

what happens at the end of a cue

You decide for each cue: it stops; it holds on the last frame until you press GO, or for a number of seconds; the next one starts; it loops (forever or N times) and then does one of the others.

Coming in and going out: a cut or a dissolve, as long as you like. "Finish" lets a loop end its current round and then do its end action. "Black" (the B key) takes everything to black in 1 s.

in and out points, fit, fill, stretch

In Preview you see the whole file and mark where it should start and end (I and O, as in editing software). For video and images you choose whether to Fit (with bars), Fill (cropping) or Stretch.

a show is a file

A show is a .cine package with a readable file inside (JSON). It opens with a double-click in the Finder, ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z undo and redo every change, and if a file has been moved it finds it again (and if it's missing, the cue turns red and GO won't start it).

05under the hood

one engine

Video and audio are read as samples and locked to the audio interface's clock. GO reaches the screen in about 2 refreshes, always the same.

the output

A full-screen window on the second screen, with no menu bar or Dock. If the cable comes out, cine notices, and when the output returns it goes back to full screen by itself.

frame rates

Each cue tells you whether its frame rate is compatible with the screen: 25p and 50p on 50 Hz run clean; 29.97p or 30p stutter.

06what it runs on (and what's missing)

  • Mac with macOS 26 or later. iPad later on, as a remote.
  • Files only: video, images and audio. Countdowns and clocks will go in another app.
  • External control (OSC, then MIDI) isn't there yet. Starts are always manual.
  • Programme audio on an interface of your choice, plus Preview listening on a second interface.
